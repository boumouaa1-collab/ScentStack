Section 1 — Getting StartedScent Stack

Welcome

◆

You are about to build something most fragrance lovers never do: a real, intentional wardrobe of scents that matches your life, your seasons, and your personality.

This workbook is not a list of perfumes to buy. It is a system for understanding what you love, why you love it, and how to build a collection that feels like you.

Whether you own two bottles or twenty, whether you are just starting out or have been wearing fragrance for years, the pages ahead will help you turn a random shelf into a curated wardrobe.

Take your time. There is no rush. Fragrance is personal, and the best discoveries happen when you pay attention.

A note from Scent Stack

This workbook is an original creation for fragrance enthusiasts. It is not affiliated with any brand. It does not make medical claims. It is a journaling and planning tool designed to enhance your personal enjoyment of fragrance.

How to Use This Workbook

◆
  • Print it — Use US Letter paper (A4 works too). Print the tracker and review pages as many copies as you need.
  • Go digital — Import the PDF into GoodNotes, Notability, or any PDF annotation app to write with a stylus on your tablet.
  • Work in order — Start with Section 1 and move through the sections. Each builds on the last.
  • Or jump around — If you already know your taste, skip ahead to the wardrobe builder or tracker sections.
  • Be honest — The worksheets only work if you are honest about what you actually like and wear.
  • Revisit pages — Your taste will evolve. Come back to your scent personality and wardrobe planning pages over time.
  • Use the trackers — The collection tracker and review pages are designed to be printed multiple times. One page per perfume.

What you will need

A pen or stylus, your current fragrance collection, and a quiet moment to think about what you actually enjoy wearing.

Section 2 — Understand Your TasteScent Stack

Fragrance Families

◆

Every fragrance belongs to a family — a category that describes its dominant character. Knowing these families helps you talk about what you like and find new fragrances faster.

Citrus

Bright, zesty, refreshing. Lemon, bergamot, grapefruit, orange. Uplifting and energizing.

Floral

Rose, jasmine, lily, peony. The largest family. Romantic, soft, or bold depending on the bloom.

Fruity

Peach, apple, berry, mango. Sweet and playful without being dessert-like.

Gourmand

Vanilla, caramel, chocolate, coffee. Edible and warm. Cozy and comforting.

Woody

Sandalwood, cedar, vetiver, oud. Dry, warm, grounding. Often unisex.

Musky

Clean or animalic musk. Soft, skin-like, intimate. Often a base for other notes.

Amber

Warm, resinous, slightly sweet. Labdanum, benzoin, ambergris. Rich and enveloping.

Spicy

Cinnamon, cardamom, pepper, clove. Warm, stimulating, often a complement to other families.

Fresh

Clean, airy, light. Often includes aldehydes or white musk. Like fresh laundry or clean skin.

Aquatic

Sea spray, salt, water notes. Breezy and cool. Like a morning by the ocean.

Green

Cut grass, leaves, stems, galbanum. Crisp and natural. Like a walk through a garden.

Chypre

Bergamot top, floral heart, mossy base. Sophisticated and structured. A classic structure.

Section 2 — Understand Your TasteScent Stack

Fragrance Notes

◆

A fragrance is built in three layers, called a pyramid. Each layer appears at a different time as the fragrance develops on your skin.

Top Notes

The first thing you smell when you spray. These are light, volatile molecules that evaporate quickly — usually within 15 to 30 minutes. Citrus, herbs, and light fruits are common top notes. First impressions matter, but do not judge a fragrance by them alone.

Heart Notes

The core of the fragrance. These appear after the top notes fade and last for several hours. Floral, fruity, and spicy notes often sit here. The heart is what most people will remember about your scent.

Base Notes

The foundation. These are heavy, slow-evaporating molecules that can last all day. Woods, musk, amber, and vanilla are typical base notes. The base is what lingers on your clothes and skin hours later.

Top → Heart → Base

30 min → 3-4 hours → 6+ hours

Section 9 — Fragrance ShoppingScent Stack

Before You Buy Checklist

◆

Run through this list every time before you purchase a new fragrance.

  • Have I tested it on my skin (not just a paper strip)?
  • Have I smelled the dry-down, not just the opening?
  • Does it fill a gap in my wardrobe?
  • Is it different enough from what I already own?
  • Can I afford it without regret?
  • Have I waited at least 24 hours since first trying it?
  • Do I love it, or am I just excited by the novelty?
  • Have I checked the price at multiple retailers?
  • Will I wear it at least once a week?
  • Does it match my scent personality?

Section 10 — 30-Day ChallengeScent Stack

30-Day Challenge — Days 1-15

◆
1List every fragrance you own. Write them all down — even samples. You cannot organize what you have not inventoried.
2Wear a forgotten fragrance. Pick the one you have not worn in the longest time. Pay attention to how it develops.
3Identify your favorite top note. Spray 2-3 fragrances and note what you smell in the first 5 minutes. What do you love?
4Test on paper vs. skin. Spray the same fragrance on a strip and your wrist. Notice the difference. Skin chemistry matters.
5Review one fragrance fully. Fill out a perfume review page for a fragrance you know well.
6Discover a new family. Visit a store and test a fragrance from a family you never wear. Just try it.
7Find your base note. Smell your wrist 4 hours after applying. What lingers? That is your base note for the day.
8Organize your collection. Arrange your fragrances by family, season, or occasion. Photograph them.
9Layer two fragrances. Apply one, wait 10 minutes, then apply another. Does it work? Log your experiment.
10Test a fragrance in a different season. Wear a winter scent on a warm day (or vice versa). Does it change?
11Write your scent personality. Complete the My Scent Personality page from Section 2.
12Identify a wardrobe gap. Which occasion do you lack a scent for? Write it down.
13Compare two similar fragrances. Spray two scents from the same family side by side. Which wins?
14Rate your most-worn fragrance. Give it an honest rating. Are you wearing it out of love or habit?
15Halfway reflection. What have you learned about your taste so far? Write it down.
50
Section 10 — 30-Day ChallengeScent Stack

30-Day Challenge — Days 16-30

◆
16Wear a sample you have been ignoring. Pull out a sample vial you never got around to testing. Give it a full day.
17Experiment with sprays. Try 2 sprays of your signature instead of your usual number. Does more or less change the scent?
18Test a fragrance on clothes. Spray on a scarf or sleeve. How does it differ from skin? Does it last longer?
19Discover a new note. Visit a store and ask for something with a note you have never tried. Learn to recognize it.
20Plan your seasonal rotation. Fill out the next season's planning page with what you want to wear.
21Layer for an occasion. Create a layering combination for a specific event (date night, work, etc.).
22Review a fragrance you dislike. Fill out a review page for it. Understanding why you dislike something teaches you about your taste.
23Audit your collection. Complete the Collection Audit page. Be honest about what you wear and what you do not.
24Identify your signature candidate. Narrow your candidates to one. Wear it for 3 days straight.
25Test longevity honestly. Time how long your fragrance actually lasts. Write down when you last smell it.
26Ask someone's opinion. Wear your signature candidate and ask a friend what they think. Listen without defending it.
27Research one fragrance. Read about a fragrance you are considering. Learn its notes, family, and what people say about its performance.
28Create a wishlist. Fill out the Perfume Wishlist page with your top 5 fragrances to buy next.
29Write your fragrance goals. What do you want your collection to look like in a year?
30Final reflection. Complete the Final Fragrance Wardrobe page. Celebrate how far your taste has come.
